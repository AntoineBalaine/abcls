## Outline

- Phase 1 replaces the three separate avoid note checks of `getAvailableTensions` by one rule: a tension is unavailable when it lies a minor second above any chord tone.
- Phase 2 makes `voiceChord` turn a major seventh chord into a major sixth chord, and a minor-major seventh chord into a minor sixth chord, when the lead is the root. It applies to every voicing and every voice count.
- Phase 3 adds a new voicing, `scaleClose`, that builds the chord from the chord scale by walking down from the lead. It works with 4, 5 and 6 voices and it is reachable with `g t` in the transform mode of the modal keymap, so that the existing voicings stay untouched.

Each phase carries its own tests, with the example cases and the properties that it needs.

## Context

The Berklee text on the five-part spread and the later discussion gave these facts:

- The book has no rule for the six-part close voicing. It defers to the chord scale voicing rules: any diatonic tension is available, provided that it is not a minor second (or a minor ninth) above a chord tone.
- A major ninth is virtual, because it folds into a major second.
- Because the diatonic ninth of IIIm7 lies a minor ninth above its root, the book does not list it, and no knowledge of the scale degree of the chord is needed.
- The #9 and the #11 of a dominant chord are available, because no chord tone lies a half step below them.
- A tension that lies a half step below a chord tone stays available.
- The lead on the root of a major seventh chord makes the voicing a major sixth chord, and the lead on the root of a minor-major seventh chord makes it a minor sixth chord. The reason is that the seventh would sit a minor second below the lead.

The current code lives in these places:

- `parse/music-theory/harmonization.ts`: `getAvailableTensions` (the avoid note checks), `getIntervals` (the sixth chord support, through `chord.extension === 6`), `buildChordScale` and `buildClusterVoicing`.
- `editor/src/transforms/harmonize.ts`: `VoicingType`, `voiceChord` (the voicing step) and `harmonizeVoicing`.
- `editor/src/runner/transformLookup.ts`: the dispatch of `harmonizeVoicing` with the arguments `[snapshots, voicing, voiceCount, degree, chordPositions]`.
- `vscode-extension/src/keymap/defaultKeymap.ts`: the `g` group of the transform mode.
- `docs-site/src/content/docs/user-guide/modal-keymap.mdx`: the table of the keys, which a generator keeps in sync with the keymap.

## Phase 1: the unified tension rule

File: `parse/music-theory/harmonization.ts`, function `getAvailableTensions`.

Today the function computes three flags: `isMinor2ndAboveThird`, `isMinor2ndAboveSeventh` and `isMinor9thAvoid`. The fifth is not checked, and the root is checked only for the ninth. The new rule replaces the three flags.

```lua
chordTonePitchClasses = pitch classes of rootPosChord[0..3]       -- root, third, fifth, seventh
for degree in 9, 11, 13
  for each chord tone
    if (tensionPitchClass - chordTonePitchClass + 12) % 12 == 1
      unavailable, except when the chord tone is the root and chord.quality is Dominant
```

Signature and imports do not change. The `ChordQuality` import is already present.

Tests, in `parse/tests/harmonization.spec.ts` (the file that holds the tension tests):

- Example cases: Dm7 in C has the 9, the 11 and the 13; Em7 in C has only the 11 (the 9 lies a half step above the root and the 13 lies a half step above the fifth); Am7 in C has no 13 (F lies a half step above the fifth E); G7 in C has the 9 and the 13 and no 11 (C lies a half step above B); Cmaj7 has the 9 and the 13 and no 11; C7 in a major key keeps the 9 and the 13.
- Property: for every chord quality, every root and every key, no available tension lies a minor second above a chord tone, except the root exception of dominant chords.

## Phase 2: the sixth chord swap

Files: `parse/music-theory/harmonization.ts` (the new helper) and `editor/src/transforms/harmonize.ts` (function `voiceChord`, before `buildChord` is called).

New helper, exported for the tests: `swapChordForRootLead(chord: ParsedChord, leadPitchClass: number): ParsedChord`. The type `ParsedChord` comes from `parse/music-theory/types.ts`.

```lua
rootPitchClass = pitch class of chord.root with chord.rootAccidental
if leadPitchClass ~= rootPitchClass
  return chord
if chord.quality == Major and chord.extension not in {6, 69}
  return chord with extension = 6
if chord.quality == MinorMajor7
  return chord with quality = Minor and extension = 6
return chord
```

`voiceChord` replaces its `chord` parameter by the swapped chord for all voicings. Because `getIntervals` already builds a sixth chord from `extension === 6` and `getAvailableTensions` already drops the 13 for it, no other function changes.

Tests, in `parse/tests/chordScaleVoicing.spec.ts` and in `editor/tests/harmonize.spec.ts` (where the old expectation for a lead on the root of `C` changes from `[EGBc]` to `[EGAc]`):

- Example cases: Cmaj7 with the lead C in 4 voices gives C A G E from the top; CmMaj7 with the lead C gives the notes of Cm6; Cmaj7 with the lead E is unchanged; C7 with the lead C is unchanged.
- Property: with any lead and any voice count, no voicing of a major seventh chord has a minor second between the lead and the voice below it.

## Phase 3: the chord scale close voicing and its keys

Files:

- `parse/music-theory/harmonization.ts`: new function `buildScaleCloseVoicing`, exported from `parse/music-theory/index.ts`.
- `editor/src/transforms/harmonize.ts`: `VoicingType` gets the value `"scaleClose"`, and `voiceChord` gets a branch for it.
- `vscode-extension/src/keymap/defaultKeymap.ts`: new leaf `t` in the group `g`.
- `docs-site/src/content/docs/user-guide/modal-keymap.mdx`: the new row, regenerated by the table generator.
- `abc-cli/commands/harmonize.ts`: `scaleClose` is added to the accepted voicings.

Signature:

```lua
buildScaleCloseVoicing(
  rootPosChord: VoicedNote[],                 -- from buildChord
  tensions: Map<9 | 11 | 13, VoicedNote>,     -- from getAvailableTensions
  leadMidi: number,
  voiceCount: 4 | 5 | 6
): VoicedNote[] | null                        -- null when the lead is not in the chord scale
```

Algorithm:

```lua
scale = chord tones, then the tensions whose pitch class is not already a chord tone
lead = the scale note with the pitch class of leadMidi, placed on leadMidi   -- null when absent
voices = {lead}
used = {pitch class of lead}
while #voices < voiceCount and an unused scale pitch class remains
  last = lowest voice
  next = highest midi below last.midi whose pitch class is an unused scale note
  if #voices == 1 and lead.midi - next.midi <= 2
    skip this candidate, and take the next one down       -- no second under the lead
  add next
while #voices < voiceCount                                -- the scale is exhausted
  make every scale note available again, and add the nearest one below the lowest voice
if the voicing has no third or no seventh                 -- seventh chord functions 3 and 7
  replace the lowest tension by the missing guide tone,
  placed at the highest midi below the voice above it
sort the voices from the lowest to the highest
```

The new branch of `voiceChord`:

```lua
if voicing == "scaleClose"
  tensions = getAvailableTensions(rootPosChord, chord, key)
  voicedChord = buildScaleCloseVoicing(rootPosChord, tensions, lead.midi, voiceCount)
  if voicedChord == null then return null
```

Tests, in `parse/tests/harmonization.spec.ts` and `editor/tests/harmonize.spec.ts`:

- Example cases: Cmaj7 with the lead E and 6 voices gives E C B A G D; Cmaj7 with the lead C and 6 voices gives C A G E D C (the chord became C6, so the B is absent and the sixth voice is a doubling); Dm7 in C with the lead D and 6 voices contains the seventh C by the guide tone repair; Cmaj7 with the lead G and 4 voices contains the seventh B; a lead outside the chord scale returns null.
- Properties: the voicing has exactly `voiceCount` voices, sorted without repeated midi values; the top voice is the lead; the gap between the lead and the second voice is greater than 2 semitones; the voicing contains the third and the seventh of the chord (the sixth for a sixth chord); every pitch class belongs to the chord scale.

Keymap tests: `vscode-extension/src/keymap/keymap.spec.ts` and `docTables.spec.ts` keep passing, and a new example case checks that `g t` resolves to `harmonizeVoicing` with the arguments `["scaleClose", count(4, 1), null]`.
