## 1. Why this work exists

`exportAbcxToIrealLink` turns ABCx source into an `irealb://` link. It works, at
the level it attempts: it reads a real ABCx tree rather than munging text, and it
refuses a chord symbol it cannot fully parse rather than exporting a truncated
one. What it attempts is the problem. Its intermediate form is a flat list of
five token kinds, so a chart exported through it keeps its chords and its
barlines and loses everything else: section labels, repeats, numbered endings,
time signatures, annotations, and the segno and coda markers. A chart that goes
out through it and comes back is a different chart, longer and formless.

Nothing has consumed it, which is why that went unnoticed. Its round-trip tests
pass because every fixture in them is one to four bars of plain chords with no
repeats, so they confirm the subset it handles and say nothing about the rest.

Two things have changed since it was written, and together they make full
fidelity straightforward rather than ambitious. The iReal grid language now has
a scanner and a parser, and `IrealChart` models every construct named above.
And the ABCx tree turns out to carry each of those constructs already, measured
rather than assumed: see section 5. What is missing is only the step that writes
an `IrealChart` back out as grid text, which is the mirror of the parser.

## 2. What exists today, and what it loses

`ireal/exportToIreal.ts`, 97 lines. It scans and parses ABCx, walks each `Tune`,
reads `T:`, `C:`, `K:`, `Q:` and the `irealstyle`, `irealgroove` and
`irealrepeats` directives for the link's fields, and walks the tune body
emitting a `GridToken` for each `ChordSymbol` and each `BarLine`. It then calls
`gridTokensToText`, `scramble` and `buildPlaylistLink`.

`ireal/gridNotation.ts`, 83 lines, holds that intermediate form. It is two halves
with opposite fates. `textToGridTokens` is the reader that the scanner and parser
replaced, and the regex cleanup in it is the original cause of the defect class
plan 2 exists to remove. `gridTokensToText` is the writer this plan replaces: its
`GridToken` is a flat union of chord, bar, no-chord, repeat-bar and
section-label, with no notion of a section, a repeat, an ending, a time signature
or an annotation, so it cannot express what this plan requires. The new writer
takes an `IrealChart` and walks its sections; it subsumes those thirteen lines
rather than extending them.

Both files, and `exportToIreal.ts` as it stands, are therefore deleted by plan 3
phase C, and this work rebuilds the export on top of the tree afterwards.
Nothing is lost in the gap: the field and scramble encoders that
`roundtrip.spec.ts` incidentally exercises have their own coverage in
`fields.spec.ts` and `scramble.spec.ts`.

## 3. The decision that shapes everything else

`IrealChart` is the intermediate form, and the only one. The export reads ABCx
into that tree and then writes the tree out as grid text; it never assembles grid
text while walking the ABCx tree.

The reason is that the two jobs fail differently and a single pass hides which
one failed. Reading ABCx is where a construct may be unrepresentable, and that
should be reported against a position in the ABCx source. Writing grid text is
where a construct may be inexpressible in iReal Pro's own language, and that is a
property of the chart rather than of anyone's source. Keeping them apart also
means the writer is testable against the parser directly, which section 9 relies
on and which a fused implementation would make impossible.

## 4. Target architecture

```
ABCx source
  -> ScannerAbcx, parseAbcx            (exists)
  -> abcxTuneToIrealChart              (phase B, new)
       -> IrealChart                   (exists, gridAst.ts)
  -> writeGrid                         (phase A, new)
       -> grid text
  -> scramble                          (exists)
  -> buildSongFieldString, buildPlaylistLink   (exists)
```

The reverse direction already exists and is `scanGrid` then `parseGrid`, which is
what makes the round trip in section 9 available as an oracle.

## 5. What the ABCx tree already carries

Measured by parsing this source and printing the nodes, not assumed:

```
[P:A]|: Cmaj7 "annot" | Dm7 :|[1 Em7 :|[2 F7 |
[P:B][M:3/4] G7 | Am7 |]
```

- A section label arrives as `Inline_field` with `field.lexeme` of `P:`.
- A time signature arrives as `Inline_field` with `M:`.
- A repeat arrives in `BarLine.barline`, whose tokens spell `|:`, `:|`, `:|[` and
  `|]` as written.
- A numbered ending arrives in `BarLine.repeatNumbers`, as the tokens `1`, `2`.
- An annotation arrives as an `Annotation` node carrying its quoted text.
- A chord arrives as `ChordSymbol`, whose lexeme `scanChordSymbol` and
  `parseChordSymbol` turn into a `ParsedChord`.

So every construct this plan promises has a source in the tree. Two have no ABCx
spelling settled yet and are listed as open questions in section 11: the segno
and coda markers, and the cue-sized chord.

## 6. Phase A: the grid writer

New file `ireal/gridWriter.ts`.

```
writeGrid(chart: IrealChart, ctx: ABCContext): string
```

It walks the tree and returns the unscrambled grid text that `scanGrid` reads.
Because the scanner's token vocabulary is already written down in
`gridTokens.ts`'s `GridTT`, the writer's job is to emit those lexemes in the
order the grammar in plan 2 section 4 permits, and that enum is the list of what
it must be able to write.

Rules, each the inverse of a parser rule:

- A section opens with `[` or, when it carries a repeat, `{`, and closes with the
  matching `]` or `}`. Plan 2 section 4 records that these are the two repeat
  barlines rather than a matched pair, which the writer must honour: a section
  that closes a repeat where the next opens one writes `}{`, not `}` `{`.
- A section label writes `*` followed by its letter, immediately after the
  opening bracket.
- A time signature writes `T` followed by its two digits.
- Bars within a section are separated by `|`.
- A cell writes its chord in iReal Pro's dialect through
  `parsedChordToIrealText`. A `noChord` cell writes `n`. An unresolved
  `sameChord` writes `p`, and `sameChordWithBass` writes `W/` and its bass.
- An alternative chord writes in parentheses after the cell it belongs to.
- A cue-sized cell writes `s` before it, a fermata `f` before its bar.
- An ending writes `N` and its number before its first bar.
- An annotation writes `<` its text `>`.
- A segno writes `S` and a coda `Q`, at the section its index names.

Two properties the writer must have, both checked in section 9. It must not emit
a construct the scanner cannot read, and it must not emit a bar the parser will
read as a different bar. The second is the stronger claim and the one the
round-trip property pins.

Tests in `ireal/gridWriter.spec.ts`: one per rule above, each asserting the exact
text, written in iReal Pro's own dialect. Minor seventh is `D-7`, never `Dm7`;
major seventh is `C^7`; half-diminished is `Ch7`. A fixture in ABC's dialect
makes correct code look broken, which has cost this work real time repeatedly.
No fixture passes through `scramble`, which substitutes on `| x`, ` |` and
three-space runs and would change it.

## 7. Phase B: reading an ABCx tune into an iReal chart

New file `ireal/abcxToIrealChart.ts`.

```
abcxTuneToIrealChart(tune: Tune, ctx: ABCContext): IrealChart
abcxTuneToSongFields(tune: Tune, ctx: ABCContext): IrealSongFields
```

The first walks the tune body and builds the tree; the second reads the header
for the link's own fields and is today's `buildTuneFields` with the grid-building
half removed. Both report through `ctx.errorReporter` rather than throwing, with
one exception kept from today's behaviour: a chord symbol that scans only
partially is an error that must stop the export, because `BbMaj7` scanning as
`Bb` would otherwise export a truncated chord silently.

The walk's own rules:

- An `Inline_field` of `P:` starts a new section and gives it that label. A tune
  with no `P:` field at all is one unlabelled section.
- An `Inline_field` of `M:` sets the current section's time signature.
- A `BarLine` whose tokens open a repeat marks the current section as repeated;
  one that closes a repeat closes it. `:|[` both closes and introduces an
  ending, which is the shape real ABC writes and the one today's tests do not
  cover.
- `BarLine.repeatNumbers` names the ending the following bars belong to.
- A `ChordSymbol` appends a cell to the current bar; a plain `BarLine` ends it.
- An `Annotation` attaches to the bar being built.

Tests in `ireal/abcxToIrealChart.spec.ts`, one per rule, with ABCx fixtures. Note
that these fixtures are ABC's dialect, the opposite of phase A's: here a minor
seventh is `Dm7`, since that is what ABCx writes. The two spec files sitting side
by side with opposite dialects is a trap worth a comment at the top of each.

## 8. Phase C: the entry point, and what it replaces

`exportAbcxToIrealLink(abcxSource: string): string` keeps its name and signature,
and becomes the composition in section 4: parse, then per tune read the fields
and the chart, write the grid, scramble, and build the link. It stays the only
entry point this work exports from the package root. The website is not an input,
per the decision in section 10, so `writeGrid` needs no root export.

`roundtrip.spec.ts` is rewritten against the new path. Its existing cases stay as
the floor, since a flat chord grid must keep working, and the cases the old
implementation could not express are added: a repeat, two numbered endings, a
section label, a time signature and an annotation each surviving the round trip.

## 9. Invariants and the verification harness

The parser gives this work an oracle that most writers do not get: for any grid
text the scanner accepts, parsing it and writing it again must produce text that
parses to the same tree. That is checkable over the whole 657-chart library,
which is real iReal Pro output rather than anything we wrote.

Extend `ireal/tools/verifyAgainstLibrary.ts`:

- Tree fixpoint. For every chart, `parseGrid(scanGrid(writeGrid(chart)))` equals
  `chart`. Equality is structural over the tree, not over text, because the
  writer is permitted to normalise spacing and the parser is permitted to resolve
  a back reference. This is the invariant that makes the writer trustworthy.
- No unknown tokens. Scanning the written text produces zero `GridTT.UNKNOWN`
  tokens, so the writer cannot emit something the scanner merely tolerates.
- Chord preservation, over `chartChords`, between the original tree and the
  reparsed one. The fixpoint subsumes it, but it fails more legibly.
- Round trip through the link. For every chart, parsing the link built from it
  recovers the same field values, which covers `scramble` and
  `buildSongFieldString` on real data rather than on fixtures.

Add a property-based spec, `ireal/gridWriter.pbt.spec.ts`, following the
generator pattern in `ireal/gridScanner.pbt.generators.ts`. The property is the
same fixpoint stated over generated trees rather than real charts, which is what
reaches the shapes the library happens not to contain.

The existing suite stays green. It is 4387 passing and 14 pending at the time of
writing, and `npm run build:ci` must pass, which is what CI runs; building only
the parse workspace is what let a broken build reach CI once already.

## 10. Decisions this plan rests on

Answered:

- Full structural fidelity, as listed in section 1. The ABCx tree carries every
  construct and the iReal tree models every construct, so the work is to connect
  them rather than to choose a subset.
- ABCx source is the only input. The website does not produce links, so no
  tree-to-link entry point is exported.
- Plan 3 phase C deletes `gridNotation.ts` and `exportToIreal.ts` first, and this
  work rebuilds the export afterwards. Keeping the old module to preserve its
  writer would mean keeping its reader, which is the regex path being removed,
  and its writer cannot express what this plan requires anyway.

## 11. Open questions

These need an answer before the phase that depends on them, not before the plan
is accepted.

- The ABCx spelling of a segno and a coda. `IrealChart.navigation` holds them as
  section indexes, and the grid writer knows how to write `S` and `Q`, but
  nothing settles how they are written in ABCx so that phase B can read them.
  ABC's own `!segno!` and `!coda!` decorations are the obvious candidate, and
  `Decoration` is already a node in the tree. Determine empirically, the way
  `[P:A]` was settled: write a candidate, parse it, and check both that the error
  reporter stays clean and that the chord after it survives.
- The ABCx spelling of a cue-sized chord, for the `s` marker. There may be none,
  in which case the writer can still emit it from a tree that has it and phase B
  simply never produces one.
- Whether `IrealChart.navigation` holding one coda index is enough. One chart in
  the sample library marks three, which `ireal/KNOWN_GAPS.md` records. If the
  export is to survive that chart, the tree needs a list rather than an index,
  which is a change in plan 2's territory rather than this one's.
